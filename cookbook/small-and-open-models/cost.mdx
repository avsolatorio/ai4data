# 7. Cost, skills, and sustainability

**Question:** What do they cost, and how is the capacity sustained?

## Rationale

A local model costs hardware, power, and people; a hosted model costs
per query and a dependency. The comparison is over years and includes
what is easy to forget: the staff time to run the server, the updates,
the suite runs, and the skills that leave when a person does. For most
statistical organizations the arithmetic favours local open models for
the narrow, high-volume tasks and hosted or large models for a few
others, and it favours sharing: one GPU server serves every unit's
batch jobs, one team maintains the registry and the suites, and
organizations share test sets and fine-tuned models where licences
allow. The sustainability plan names the people, the budget lines, and
the renewal dates.

## Target state

- The cost of each task is known per query under its chosen model, including staff time, and compared yearly with the alternatives.
- A small team with named roles maintains the registry, the serving, the suites, and the adaptations, with the skills documented and shared.
- Hardware and software renewals, model reviews, and budget lines are planned over three years, and shared infrastructure is used where it exists.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Estimate the three-year cost of the first local deployment against hosted use; name the person who runs it. |
| **AI-ready** | Measure cost per query per task in production; staff the roles; plan renewals and reviews; share the server across units. |
| **AI-native** | Share infrastructure, test sets, and fine-tuned models across organizations; report cost and capacity yearly with the AI component register. |

:::info[Running example]
The example organization runs its coding, extraction, and embedding
tasks on one 24 GB GPU server inside the production network and uses a
hosted model for release commentary and the public assistant. Its
three-year estimate puts the server, power, and a half-time engineer
against the hosted alternative for the same volumes; the local tasks
cost a fraction per query, and the hosted tasks stay hosted because
their volume is low and their quality requirement high.
:::

## Recipes

<Recipe title="7.1 Estimate the total cost over three years" level="Foundational" skills="Head of IT; finance" time="Half a day">

**Result:** a three-year cost of the local deployment against hosted
use for the same tasks and volumes, with the assumptions written down.

1. Local: hardware (the server from [chapter 3](./serving.mdx), amortized
   over three years), power and hosting, the staff share (an engineer's
   fraction for running and updating), and the suite runs' time.
2. Hosted: the measured tokens per query times prices for each task's
   volume, plus the staff share for integration and monitoring, plus the
   cost of the data that cannot be sent (the tasks that would have to
   stay manual).
3. Compare per task and in total; the frontier of
   [chapter 5](./comparison.mdx) gives the per-query figures.
4. Record the assumptions (volumes, prices, salaries, lifetime) with the
   estimate and revisit yearly; prices and volumes both change.

**What this does not do.** An estimate with written assumptions is a
plan; the measured cost per query after a year of production replaces
it, as the [production cookbook](/cookbook/language-models-in-production/running)
measures batch jobs.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="7.2 Staff the roles and document the skills" level="AI-ready" skills="Head of the unit" time="Half a day">

**Result:** named people for the roles the capacity needs, with the
skills documented so that the capacity survives a departure.

1. Roles: a model engineer (registry, serving, updates, verification);
   an evaluation owner (suites, gates, report cards); a task owner per
   adapted task (prompts, data, decisions); a security contact. One
   person can hold two roles; no role is unassigned.
2. Document the procedures as runbooks: importing a model, verifying
   and deploying it, running the suite, rolling back; the scripts of
   these cookbooks are the runbooks' core.
3. Train a second person per role; the switch-off test of the
   [dissemination cookbook](/cookbook/ai-ready-dissemination/sustain)
   doubles as a staffing test when run by the second person.
4. Share training with other organizations through the program's
   events and materials.

**What this does not do.** Roles and runbooks keep the capacity
running; they do not make the organization a research lab. The scope
stays the narrow tasks the frontier favours, and the ambitious ones use
hosted or shared capacity.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="7.3 Share infrastructure, test sets, and models" level="AI-native" skills="Head of IT; head of the unit" time="Ongoing">

**Result:** one serving capacity for the organization's units, and test
sets and fine-tuned models shared with other organizations where
licences and data allow.

1. Inside the organization: one model service with quotas per unit
   ([chapter 3](./serving.mdx)); one registry; one evaluation owner;
   cost allocated by measured use.
2. Across organizations: publish question sets whose content is public
   ([evaluation cookbook, chapter 9](/cookbook/evaluation-suites/reporting));
   share fine-tuned models whose training data allow it, with cards;
   use shared or pooled infrastructure where a regional or program
   arrangement exists.
3. Record what is shared and under which terms in the AI component
   register.

**What this does not do.** Sharing reduces cost and raises quality for
tasks with common structure (coding to international classifications,
catalog search); tasks on national data and language remain the
organization's to adapt.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Cost model:** amortized hardware, power, staff share, suite time against measured hosted spend; per task and in total; assumptions recorded.
- **Roles:** model engineer, evaluation owner, task owners, security contact; runbooks from the cookbooks' scripts; a second person per role.
- **Sharing:** one service and registry per organization; public question sets and shareable fine-tuned models across organizations; pooled infrastructure where available.

## Common mistakes

- **Hardware without the engineer.** The server idles and the models age.
- **Hosted costs without the data that cannot be sent.** Those tasks stay manual, which is a cost.
- **One person who knows everything.** The capacity leaves with them.
- **Every unit with its own server.** One service, quotas, measured use.

## Verification

- **Cost:** a three-year estimate with written assumptions, revisited within the year; measured cost per query per task in production.
- **Roles:** named, with runbooks and a trained second person; the switch-off test run by the second person.
- **Sharing:** one service and registry per organization; shared assets recorded with terms.
