# 2. Candidate models and licences

**Question:** Which models are candidates, and under which licences?

## Rationale

Open-weight models are published by companies, research groups, and
communities under licences that range from permissive (Apache 2.0, MIT)
to open-weight licences with use restrictions and acceptable-use
policies, and some that allow reading the weights and forbid using them. The
differences matter to a public organization: whether the model may be
used for the organization's purposes, modified, redistributed inside
the organization, and run for others. A candidate is a model whose
licence a person has read and found acceptable, whose model card states
what the organization needs to know (languages, training data,
evaluation, limitations), and whose weights can be verified. The
shortlist is small, by design, because every candidate is run through
the suite.

## Target state

- Candidate models are found through a few named sources and shortlisted by language coverage, size class, and licence.
- Every candidate's licence is read and classified (permissive, restricted, or unusable) by a named person, with the restrictions recorded.
- Every candidate has a model card that passes the organization's check, and its weights have a recorded digest.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Shortlist two or three candidates per size class from named sources; read and classify their licences. |
| **AI-ready** | Check each candidate's model card; record digests; keep the shortlist in a registry with the licence classification. |
| **AI-native** | Review the shortlist on each notable release; maintain the organization's own fine-tuned models with their own cards. |

:::info[Running example]
The example organization keeps a shortlist of six candidates for its
coding task: three local open-weight models of 3, 8, and 14 billion
parameters, a large local one, and two hosted ones. Its own fine-tuned
3-billion coder has a model card that passes the check: licence,
languages, training data that never left the organization, an
evaluation with a date and numbers, limitations, hardware, contact.
:::

## Recipes

<Recipe title="2.1 Find candidates from named sources and shortlist" level="Foundational" skills="Data scientist" time="Half a day">

**Result:** a shortlist of two or three candidates per size class for
each task family, with language coverage and licence noted.

1. Sources: model hubs with licence and language filters, the
   leaderboards for generation and for embeddings (with care, since
   public benchmarks are not the organization's suite), and the models
   the program's work has measured ([Efficient and Inclusive AI](/docs/inclusive-ai/)).
2. Filter by: the national language present in the training data or
   the evaluation; the size classes the hardware allows
   ([chapter 3](./serving.mdx)); a licence the organization can accept
   (recipe 2.2); an active publisher with versioned releases.
3. Shortlist two or three per class; more candidates cost more suite
   runs than they return.
4. Record the shortlist with source, version, and date.

**What this does not do.** Leaderboard scores on public benchmarks
predict the suite's results loosely at best; they shortlist, and the
suite ranks.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="2.2 Read and classify the licence" level="Foundational" skills="Legal; data scientist" time="An hour per candidate">

**Result:** each candidate's licence classified for the organization's
uses, with the restrictions recorded.

1. Read the licence text and any acceptable-use policy; summarize what
   it permits and restricts for: use by a public body, modification
   (fine-tuning), internal redistribution, serving outputs to the
   public, and attribution requirements.
2. Classify: permissive (Apache 2.0, MIT, similar), restricted
   (open-weight licences with use-based restrictions or user-count
   thresholds; responsible AI licences with behavioural clauses), or
   not usable for the organization.
3. Record the classification, the restrictions, the reader, and the
   date with the candidate; a licence change in a new version triggers
   a re-read.
4. For the organization's own fine-tuned models, choose a licence for
   the derived weights that the base licence allows.

**What this does not do.** A classification is the organization's
reading for its own uses; it is not legal advice to anyone else, and
the restrictions are checked against each new use.

</Recipe>

1. For a published model, fill the card from its published model card
   (identifier, version, digest, licence, languages, training data
   statement, evaluation, limitations, hardware); where the publisher
   does not state something, the card says so and the gap is a mark
   against the candidate.
2. For the organization's own fine-tuned model, write the card as the
   publisher: the training data statement says what was used and that
   it stayed inside the organization.
3. Run the check; a failing card is completed or the candidate is
   dropped.

**What this does not do.** The check verifies presence. The evaluation
numbers on a card are reproduced on the organization's suite
([chapter 5](./comparison.mdx)) before they count.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Sources:** model hubs with licence and language metadata; generation and embedding leaderboards; the program's measurements.
- **Licence classes:** permissive; restricted (use-based, threshold-based, behavioural); not usable.
- **Cards:** the Hugging Face model card format (Markdown with YAML metadata) or the JSON of this chapter; the check before the suite.
- **Registry:** a table of candidates with version, digest, licence class, card status, and suite results.

## World Bank examples

- [Efficient and Inclusive AI Applications](/docs/inclusive-ai/) names the open-weight models the program measured for multilingual embedding and generation tasks.
- The [PI-FT toolkit](/pift-toolkit/pipeline) fine-tunes open embedding models and publishes the results, a derived model with its own documentation.

## Resources

- [Hugging Face model cards](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/model-cards): the card format with licence, language, dataset, and evaluation metadata, and the annotated template.
- [Model cards for model reporting (Mitchell et al., 2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993): the sections a card should have.
- [Responsible AI Licenses](https://www.licenses.ai/): the behavioural-use licence family and its clauses.
- [Open LLM Leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/open-llm-leaderboard/open_llm_leaderboard) and [MTEB](https://huggingface.co/spaces/mteb/leaderboard): public scores for shortlisting generation and embedding models.
- [Model card example and check](pathname:///cookbook-files/small-and-open-models/model_card_check.py): the running example.

## Common mistakes

- **A licence nobody read.** Restrictions appear at deployment.
- **Shortlists of twenty.** Every candidate costs a suite run.
- **Leaderboards as evaluation.** They shortlist; the suite ranks.
- **A fine-tuned model without a card.** The organization is now the publisher.

## Verification

- **Shortlist:** two or three candidates per size class with source, version, and date.
- **Licences:** each classified by a named reader with restrictions recorded.
- **Cards:** each passes the check; digests recorded.
