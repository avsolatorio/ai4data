# 2. Choice of synthesis method

**Question:** Which synthesis method fits the data?

## Rationale

Synthesis methods differ in what they preserve, what they need, and
what they can leak. Sequential methods fit one variable at a time
conditional on the previous ones (classification and regression trees
or parametric models) and are transparent, fast, and well understood in
official statistics. Copula methods fit the marginals and a dependence
structure. Generative networks and transformer-based generators learn
the joint distribution from the records and handle mixed types and
relational structure, at the cost of training time, tuning, and a
tendency to memorize if left unchecked. The choice follows the data (how
many records and variables, which types, whether tables are linked) and
the uses (which relationships have to hold); the running example shows
what the simplest method loses.

## Target state

- The method for each synthetic file is chosen by a written rule from the data and the intended uses, with the alternatives considered recorded.
- The synthesis runs with a recorded seed and parameters, on the organization's infrastructure, with the real data never leaving it.
- The first run is measured ([chapters 3](./utility.mdx) and [4](./risk.mdx)) before any choice is final.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Synthesize one file with a sequential method (synthpop or equivalent) with the seed recorded; measure it. |
| **AI-ready** | Compare two methods on the same file with the utility and risk measures; choose by the written rule; record parameters and alternatives in the synthesis record. |
| **AI-native** | Use a relational generator for multi-table surveys; tune by the organization's target analyses; re-synthesize at each release as a pipeline stage. |

:::info[Running example]
The 300-record extract is synthesized by the crudest method, sampling
each column independently with income conditioned on education, to show
what a method that ignores relationships loses: the marginals match, the
link between sex and labour force status disappears, and the employment
rate for men falls from 0.56 to 0.40. A sequential CART synthesis is the
method the chapter recommends as the first real attempt.
:::

## Recipes

<Recipe title="2.1 Choose the method by data and use" level="Foundational" skills="Methodologist" time="Half a day">

**Result:** the method for a file, chosen by a written rule, with the
alternatives recorded.

| Data and use | First choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| One table, up to a few hundred thousand records, mixed types, uses need marginals and main relationships | Sequential synthesis (CART) | Transparent, fast, handles mixed types, measures built in |
| One table, mostly numeric, uses need correlations | Copula (Gaussian copula) | Fits marginals and dependence directly |
| One table, many records, complex interactions, uses need higher-order structure | Transformer-based generator (REaLTabFormer) or a tabular generative network | Learns the joint distribution; needs tuning and memorization checks |
| Several linked tables (households and persons, firms and establishments) | Relational generator (REaLTabFormer relational mode, HMA) | Models parent-child structure ([chapter 5](./relational.mdx)) |
| Panel or longitudinal records | Sequential synthesis by wave with the previous wave as predictor, or a sequence model | Preserves within-person consistency |

1. Describe the data: records, variables by type, linked tables, panel
   structure; and the uses: the analyses that must give similar
   answers ([chapter 1](./purpose.mdx)).
2. Pick the first choice from the table and one alternative; both will
   be measured.
3. Record the choice and the reason in the synthesis record
   ([chapter 6](./release.mdx)).

**What this does not do.** The table gives a first choice; the measures
decide. A file whose uses need one relationship the method does not
preserve is a file whose record says so.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="2.2 Run a sequential synthesis with the parameters recorded" level="Foundational" skills="Methodologist with R or Python" time="One day for the first file">

**Result:** a synthetic file produced by a sequential method with the
visit sequence, the method per variable, the seed, and the smoothing
recorded.

1. Set the visit sequence: variables in the order that makes the
   conditioning sensible (geography, demographics, then outcomes), with
   derived variables computed after synthesis from their inputs, never
   synthesized.
2. Choose the method per variable (CART for most; parametric where the
   variable is simple); set a minimum leaf size so that no leaf is one
   person; add smoothing to continuous variables.
3. Run with a recorded seed; synthpop's `syn()` or an equivalent
   Python implementation; keep the real data on the organization's
   infrastructure.
4. Apply the rules of the survey after synthesis (ages of children
   below the head's, hours only for the employed), or synthesize within
   the rules; count what the rules changed.

**What this does not do.** Sequential synthesis preserves the
relationships along the visit sequence and attenuates the rest; the
utility report shows which. Rare categories and small areas are the
first to lose fidelity, and the record says so.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="2.3 Run a transformer-based or network generator with memorization checks" level="AI-ready" skills="Data scientist with Python and a GPU" time="Two to three days for the first file">

**Result:** a synthetic file from a generator that learns the joint
distribution, with the training stopped before memorization and the
parameters recorded.

1. Install the generator ([REaLTabFormer](https://github.com/worldbank/REaLTabFormer):
   `pip install realtabformer`) and fit it on the real table; it trains
   a sequence model over the records and includes a stopping criterion
   that measures whether generated records are too close to the
   training ones.
2. Set the sampling parameters and the seed; generate the same number
   of records as the real file, or more where the uses need it.
3. Run the risk checks of [chapter 4](./risk.mdx) first: exact copies
   and closest-record distance; a generator that copied is retrained
   with a stronger stopping rule or a smaller model.
4. Record the model version, the training parameters, the stopping
   statistic, and the seed.

**What this does not do.** A generator needs records to learn from; a
small survey (a few thousand records) is usually better served by
sequential synthesis. Training time and tuning are real costs, and the
copying check is not optional.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Sequential:** synthpop (R) with CART or parametric methods; equivalent Python implementations.
- **Copula and networks:** SDV (Gaussian copula, CTGAN, TVAE; Business Source License) for comparison; tabular generative networks where licensing allows.
- **Transformer-based:** REaLTabFormer (MIT) for tabular and relational data, with its built-in memorization stopping criterion.
- **Rules:** survey edit rules applied after synthesis or enforced during it; derived variables recomputed.

## World Bank examples

- [REaLTabFormer](https://github.com/worldbank/REaLTabFormer) (Solatorio and Dupriez, 2023) is the program's generator for tabular and relational data, with a target-based stopping criterion that limits memorization.
- The program's [GSBPM mapping](/docs/gsbpm-mapping) places synthetic data under disclosure control, where the method choice is recorded with the file.

## Resources

- [synthpop](https://cran.r-project.org/package=synthpop) and its [paper (Nowok, Raab, and Dibben, 2016)](https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v074.i11): sequential synthesis with CART and the utility and disclosure measures that come with it.
- [REaLTabFormer paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02041): the transformer-based method for tabular and relational data and its memorization control.
- [SDV](https://github.com/sdv-dev/SDV): copula, network, and multi-table synthesizers for comparison (check the licence for the organization's use).
- [UNECE starter guide](https://unece.org/statistics/publications/synthetic-data-official-statistics-starter-guide): the method overview written for statistical organizations.

## Common mistakes

- **A generator for a small survey.** Sequential methods do better with thousands of records and are transparent.
- **Derived variables synthesized.** They stop agreeing with their inputs; recompute them.
- **No seed, no parameters.** The file cannot be reproduced or explained.
- **Training until the loss stops falling.** The generator memorizes; stop by the copying check.

## Verification

- **Choice:** recorded with the reason and the alternative considered.
- **Run:** seed, parameters, visit sequence or training settings recorded; real data stayed on the organization's infrastructure.
- **Rules:** survey rules applied and the changes counted.
