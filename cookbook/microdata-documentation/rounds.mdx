# 6. Comparability across survey rounds

**Question:** Can variables be followed across survey rounds and related surveys?

## Rationale

The most common question asked of a repeated survey is a trend: has
unemployment risen since last year, has the share of children in school
changed. Answering it needs the same variable in two rounds, with the same
meaning and the same codes, or a documented correspondence where they
differ. Each round is documented separately, and the differences between
rounds (a renamed variable, a recoded education question, a dropped item)
are what a user, or an AI system, has to know before comparing. Undocumented,
these differences produce confident wrong trends.

A crosswalk between rounds is a short table, cheap to produce from two
dictionaries, and it is the piece of documentation that most directly
prevents the wrong answer.

## Target state

- Every pair of successive rounds has a crosswalk: which variables are the same, recoded, renamed, dropped, or new, with the breaks in series marked.
- Code lists that changed have a correspondence table, and the series the organization publishes state which rounds are comparable.
- Variables that measure the same concept carry the same name across rounds, or a harmonized name is published beside the original.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Produce a crosswalk between the latest two rounds and publish it with the study records. |
| **AI-ready** | Keep crosswalks for every pair of rounds; publish correspondence tables for recoded variables; record breaks in the `version_statement` and the quality statement; link related studies in the records. |
| **AI-native** | Maintain harmonized variables across rounds with reusable definitions (DDI Lifecycle or a question bank), so that a search for a concept returns the variable in every round with its comparability stated. |

:::info[Running example]
The dictionaries of `EX-LFS-2024-Q4` and `EX-LFS-2025-Q2`. Twelve
variables are the same; the education variable was recoded from four
national levels to six ISCED levels; the job search variable was renamed;
a second-job item was dropped; an informality flag and a job search methods
item are new. The crosswalk records each, and the education recoding gets a
correspondence table.
:::

Output for the running example:

```text

16 variables in lfs_2024q4_dictionary.csv, 17 in lfs_2025q2_dictionary.csv
same      12  hhid, pid, region, urban, sex, age, lfs_status, occupation, industry, hours, income, weight
recoded    2  relationship, educ
renamed    1  looked_work
dropped    1  secondjob
new        2  informal, q17b

```

1. Run the script on the two dictionaries and read the crosswalk
   ([round_crosswalk.csv](pathname:///cookbook-files/microdata-documentation/round_crosswalk.csv)).
   The warning on the duplicated name is the planted defect of
   [chapter 3](./variables.mdx); the "recoded" row for `relationship`
   is the missing code list of the later round, a defect, and the row for
   `educ` is a real recoding.
2. Review every row that is not "same": confirm the renamed match, decide
   whether a recoded variable is comparable after a correspondence
   (recipe 6.2) or is a break, and note why a variable was dropped.
3. Publish the reviewed crosswalk with the later round's record (as a
   related material or a documentation file) and state the breaks in its
   `version_statement` and quality statement ([chapter 7](./fitness.mdx)).

**What this does not do.** The script compares names, labels, questions,
and code lists. A variable whose codes stayed and whose question wording
changed is "same" to the script and may be a break; the methodologist's
review of question wording across rounds is the check.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="6.2 Publish a correspondence table for a recoded variable" level="AI-ready" skills="Subject-matter statistician; classification specialist" time="Half a day per variable">

**Result:** a table that maps each code of the earlier list to the codes of
the later list, with the relation stated, so that a user can produce a
comparable series.

1. For each recoded variable, list the old codes and the new codes and
   state the relation of each pair: exact, old splits into several new,
   several old merge into one new, or no correspondence. For the running
   example, old `2=Secondary` splits into new `2=Lower secondary` and
   `3=Upper secondary`; old `3=Tertiary` merges `4=Post-secondary
   non-tertiary` and `5=Tertiary` only approximately, which is a break at
   the fine level and comparable at the coarse level.
2. Publish the table as a file with the record and, where the codes follow
   a standard classification, as an [XKOS](https://ddialliance.org/xkos)
   correspondence with the classification's URIs
   ([chapter 4](./concepts.mdx)).
3. State in the record which level of aggregation is comparable across the
   two rounds, so that a published series can say "comparable at three
   education levels from 2024".

**What this does not do.** A correspondence makes codes comparable; it
does not make estimates comparable when the sample design or the
question wording also changed. The quality statement names those changes.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="6.3 Harmonize variable names and definitions across rounds" level="AI-native" skills="Methodologist; data curator" time="One to two weeks for a survey program">

**Result:** each concept measured in every round has one harmonized
variable, defined once, with the round-specific variables mapped to it and
the comparability of each round stated.

1. Choose the concepts that users follow over time (labour force status,
   education level, hours, income) and define a harmonized variable for
   each: name, definition, code list, and the rounds in which it is
   comparable.
2. Map every round's variable to the harmonized one through the
   crosswalks and correspondence tables; where a round is not comparable,
   say so in the mapping.
3. Hold the definitions as reusable objects (DDI Lifecycle, or the
   question bank of [chapter 4](./concepts.mdx), recipe 4.3) and reference
   them from each round's record, so that a concept search returns the
   variable in every round with its comparability.
4. Publish the harmonized file or the mapping; the
   [IPUMS International](https://international.ipums.org/international/)
   documentation is the reference example of harmonized variables with
   per-sample comparability notes.

**What this does not do.** Harmonization is a methodological decision per
concept, and a harmonized variable hides the detail of each round. The
original variables stay published beside it.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Crosswalk:** the script in this chapter; the Metadata Editor's variable list export for each round as input; the reviewed crosswalk published as a related material.
- **Correspondence tables:** CSV at the foundational level; XKOS with classification URIs where a standard is involved.
- **Harmonized variables:** DDI Lifecycle reusable variables and questions; a question bank; a harmonized data file with its own record linked to the rounds.
- **Record links:** the `related_studies` and `related_materials` elements of the schema to link rounds to each other and to the crosswalk.

## World Bank examples

- The [microdata schema](https://github.com/worldbank/metadata-schemas) carries `version_statement`, `related_studies`, and `related_materials`, where breaks and crosswalks are recorded.
- The program's Global Question Bank workstream develops reusable question and concept definitions, the basis of harmonization across rounds ([program overview](/)).
- The [Microdata Library](https://microdata.worldbank.org/) links rounds of the same survey program through its series pages.

## Resources

- [DDI Lifecycle 3.3](https://ddialliance.org/Specification/DDI-Lifecycle/3.3/): reusable variables, questions, and concepts across studies, the standard form of harmonized definitions.
- [XKOS](https://ddialliance.org/xkos): correspondence tables between classifications and code lists.
- [IPUMS International](https://international.ipums.org/international/): harmonized census and survey variables with per-sample comparability notes, the reference example for recipe 6.3.
- [ILO harmonized labour force indicators (ILOSTAT)](https://ilostat.ilo.org/): comparability notes on labour force concepts across countries and rounds, for the labour force example.
- [UN classifications registry](https://unstats.un.org/unsd/classifications/): the current versions of ISCED, ISCO, ISIC, and their correspondence tables between versions.

## Common mistakes

- **Comparing by name.** Same name, different codes, wrong trend.
- **Breaks in the methodology report only.** The record and the crosswalk are what users and systems read.
- **One crosswalk for the whole program.** Each pair of rounds has its own; changes accumulate.
- **Harmonized variables that replace the originals.** Users who need the detail lose it.

## Verification

- **Crosswalk:** exists for every pair of successive rounds, reviewed, and published with the later record.
- **Correspondence:** every recoded variable has a table and a stated comparable level.
- **Record:** breaks appear in `version_statement` and the quality statement; rounds are linked.
- **Trend test:** a question asking for a trend across the two rounds is answered with the comparability stated (the question sets of [chapter 5](./navigate.mdx)).
