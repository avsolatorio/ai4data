# 1. Findability of surveys

**Question:** Can people and AI find the survey?

## Rationale

A survey that exists only as a report and a data file on a shared drive is
invisible to search engines, to catalog harvesters, and to AI systems. The
study-level record is what all of them read: the title, the abstract, the
collection dates, the coverage, the universe, and the sampling. When the
record follows a published schema and sits in a catalog, it can be found by
a person typing a question, harvested by another catalog, and cited with a
persistent identifier.

Microdata have a second findability problem that indicators do not: users
look for a variable ("age of household head") as often as for a survey.
That part is handled in [chapter 5](./navigate.mdx); this chapter makes the
survey itself findable.

## Target state

- Every survey has a study record in the World Bank microdata schema (DDI Codebook), with the fields of the recommended profile filled.
- The record is published in a catalog that exposes it to crawlers and harvesters, with schema.org markup on the page.
- The survey has a persistent identifier, with a DOI where the organization can register one.
- The record is kept current across versions and editions.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Write a study record with title, abstract, collection dates, country, universe, analysis unit, sampling, and citation requirement. Publish it on a page with a permanent URL. |
| **AI-ready** | Store the record in the World Bank microdata schema, through the Metadata Editor or as JSON. Publish it in NADA or an equivalent catalog that emits schema.org markup. Register a DOI. Check the record against the completeness profile. |
| **AI-native** | Keep the record current through versions, expose the variables to variable-level search ([chapter 5](./navigate.mdx)), and let the catalog's API and agent interface serve the record to software. |

:::info[Running example]
The study record for `EX-LFS-2025-Q2` already passes the schema and the
foundational profile. The recipes here show what the record contains, how
it is validated, and what the AI-ready profile still asks for.
:::

## Recipes

<Recipe title="1.1 Write the study record in the World Bank microdata schema" level="Foundational" skills="Data curator; the Metadata Editor or a text editor" time="Half a day per survey">

**Result:** a study record that a catalog can import and that passes the
schema and the organization's completeness profile.

1. Document the survey in the [Metadata Editor](https://github.com/worldbank/metadata-editor),
   which has a template for microdata, validates the record, and exports
   the JSON. Alternatively, write the JSON directly; the running example is
   a minimal record:

```json file=../../website/static/cookbook-files/ai-ready-dissemination/example_microdata.json
```

2. Fill at least the fields of the foundational level of the
   [recommended microdata profile](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-ready-dissemination/profile_microdata.json):
   identifier, title, authoring entity, abstract, collection dates, nation,
   universe, analysis unit, sampling procedure, citation requirement, and
   a contact.
3. Validate the record with the metadata checker from the dissemination
   cookbook, which checks structure against the schema, completeness
   against the profile, and the validity of dates and URLs:

```bash
python check_metadata.py example_microdata.json profile_microdata.json
```

   Output for the running example: valid against `microdata-schema.json`,
   complete at the foundational level, no validity issues. At the AI-ready
   level the same record reports `data_files` and `variables` missing; those
   come from [chapter 3](./variables.mdx).

4. Keep the record in version control next to the data documentation, and
   re-run the check whenever it changes.

**What this does not do.** The check verifies that fields are present and
well-formed. Whether the abstract describes the survey correctly, whether
the sampling text is accurate, and whether the universe matches the
questionnaire are review tasks; the [Metadata Reviewer](/docs/metadata-reviewer/overview)
puts AI-generated suggestions on such text in front of a curator.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="1.2 Publish the record in a catalog with a persistent identifier" level="AI-ready" skills="Catalog administrator" time="Two hours per survey once the catalog exists">

**Result:** the survey is visible to crawlers, harvesters, and AI systems,
and citable by a persistent identifier.

1. Import the record into [NADA](https://nada.ihsn.org/) (or an equivalent
   DDI-aware catalog). NADA stores the record in the World Bank schema,
   writes a schema.org `Dataset` block on the study page, exposes the
   record through its REST API, and manages access requests.
2. Register a DOI through [DataCite](https://schema.datacite.org/), as the
   World Bank [Microdata Library](https://microdata.worldbank.org/) does
   (prefix 10.48529), and put it in the record's `datacite` block and in
   the citation requirement.
3. Give the study page a permanent URL and add it to the site map. If the
   organization runs its own site, add schema.org markup as in
   [recipe 1.2 of the dissemination cookbook](/cookbook/ai-ready-dissemination/find).
4. Verify with a structured-data validator that the page's record is
   parsed, and search for the survey's title in a general search engine
   after a few weeks.

**What this does not do.** Catalog publication makes the survey findable by
title and abstract. Users who search for a variable will still not find it
until the variables are documented ([chapter 3](./variables.mdx)) and
searchable ([chapter 5](./navigate.mdx)).

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Record schema:** the World Bank microdata schema is DDI Codebook 2.5 expressed as JSON Schema. Organizations that already produce DDI XML can convert; the Metadata Editor imports it.
- **Catalog:** NADA is open source and used by the Microdata Library and many national data archives. Any catalog that stores DDI and emits schema.org or DCAT serves the same purpose.
- **Identifiers:** DataCite DOIs are the common choice for datasets. Where a DOI is not available, a persistent URL under the organization's domain with a stated policy against change is the minimum.
- **Harvesting:** DCAT or OAI-PMH exports let other catalogs harvest the record; NADA's API serves the same need.

## World Bank examples

- The [Microdata Library](https://microdata.worldbank.org/) publishes thousands of studies in NADA with DDI records and DataCite DOIs.
- The [metadata schemas](https://github.com/worldbank/metadata-schemas) repository holds the microdata schema and a Python library for building records.
- The [Metadata Editor](https://github.com/worldbank/metadata-editor) documents microdata with templates and validation and publishes to NADA.

## Resources

- [Metadata Editor user and practice guide](https://worldbank.github.io/metadata-editor-docs): documenting a study from the microdata template and publishing it to NADA.
- [NADA](https://nada.ihsn.org/): the open-source catalog that stores DDI records, exposes schema.org and an API, and manages access types.
- [IHSN survey catalog](https://catalog.ihsn.org/): the central catalog of national surveys, which harvests national catalogs; a target for exchange.
- [DDI Codebook 2.5](https://ddialliance.org/Specification/DDI-Codebook/2.5/): the study-level elements the schema maps to.
- [schema.org Dataset](https://schema.org/Dataset) and [Google's dataset structured-data guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/dataset): what a crawler reads from the study page.
- [DataCite](https://datacite.org/): DOI registration for datasets; the Microdata Library's DOIs are DataCite DOIs.

## Common mistakes

- **A report in place of a record.** A PDF report cannot be harvested, searched by field, or cited by a persistent identifier.
- **Study identifiers that change between editions.** Each edition is a new study with its own identifier; the series links them.
- **An abstract that describes the organization.** The abstract describes what the survey measures, for whom, and when.
- **Registering a DOI without a landing page.** The DOI must resolve to the study page, which must stay at its URL.

## Verification

- **Schema and profile:** the metadata checker reports the record valid and complete at the target level.
- **Catalog page:** the study page carries a schema.org record that a validator parses.
- **Identifier:** the DOI resolves to the study page.
- **Known-item search:** the survey appears in the top results for its title and for two paraphrases of its subject.
