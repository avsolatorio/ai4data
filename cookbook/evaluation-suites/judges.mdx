# 6. Validation of automatic graders

**Question:** How are automatic graders validated?

## Rationale

A rubric applied by readers scales to thirty answers a month; a language
model applied as a grader scales to every answer of every run. The
grader is a second system that can be wrong, and it is wrong in
characteristic ways: it passes long, confident answers more often than
readers do, it prefers the first of two answers it is shown, and it
agrees with itself across runs while disagreeing with people. None of
this makes graders unusable; it makes validation a requirement. A grader
is validated against human scores on a sample, its biases are measured
and corrected in the rubric, a share of its grades is reviewed every run,
and it is validated again whenever the rubric or the model changes.

## Target state

- Every grader in use has a validation record: agreement and kappa with human scores on at least a hundred items per task, false-pass and false-fail rates, and bias checks.
- The rubric contains the rules that the bias checks required (a length rule, randomized positions).
- A share of grades is reviewed by a person each run, and the grader is re-validated on every rubric or model change.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Use readers for the rubric; no grader. |
| **AI-ready** | Validate a grader on a hundred human-scored items per task; check length and position bias; review a share of grades per run. |
| **AI-native** | Re-validate automatically when the rubric or the model changes; track grader agreement over time; use two graders where they disagree as a signal for review. |

:::info[Running example]
Twenty answers scored by a reader and by a grader. They agree on
fifteen; the grader passes four answers the reader failed and fails one
the reader passed. The bias check shows the grader passing every long
answer while the reader passed six in ten, and the rubric gains a
length rule.
:::

1. Have readers score a hundred answers per task with the rubric
   ([chapter 4](./answers.mdx)), two readers on a part of them so that
   their own agreement is known ([chapter 2](./questions.mdx)).
2. Run the grader on the same answers with the same rubric; run the
   script.
3. Read the false passes first: a grader that passes what readers fail
   lets errors through the gate. Read the bias groups: a grader that
   passes long answers more than readers do needs a rubric rule that
   length is not evidence; a position effect needs randomized order.
4. Fix the rubric or the prompt, re-run, and keep the validation record
   with the grader's version.

**What this does not do.** Agreement with readers is bounded by the
readers' agreement with each other. A grader that matches one reader at
0.85 when two readers match at 0.85 is as good as a reader, and no
better.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="6.2 Write the grader's rubric and prompt to resist its biases" level="AI-ready" skills="Developer; subject-matter staff" time="Half a day">

**Result:** a grading prompt that states the rubric's questions, the
evidence the grader may use, and the rules the bias checks required.

1. Give the grader the question, the retrieved records, and the answer,
   and ask for each rubric question as a yes-or-no with a one-line
   reason, as structured output; never a single overall score.
2. State the rules: length is not evidence; an answer is right only if
   the number appears in the retrieved records; a missing caveat that
   the metadata has is a fail; the order of answers carries no
   information.
3. Randomize the order where answers are compared, and grade each
   answer alone where possible.
4. Keep the prompt and rubric under version control; a change
   triggers recipe 6.3.

**What this does not do.** A prompt reduces the biases the organization
measured. New biases appear with new models, which is why the
validation is repeated.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="6.3 Review a share each run and re-validate on change" level="AI-ready" skills="Readers; developer" time="An hour per run">

**Result:** each run's grades carry a reviewed share with the agreement
on it, and a change to the rubric or the model re-runs the validation
before the grader is used.

1. Each run, a reader scores a random fifth of the graded answers (all
   of the failed ones where few); record the agreement on that share
   with the run.
2. When the agreement on the share drops below the validation's, stop
   using the grader's grades for the gate until re-validated.
3. On any change to the rubric, the prompt, or the model, re-run recipe
   6.1 before the grader grades a gate run.
4. Where two graders are available, use their disagreement as a signal
   for human review; averaging them hides it.

**What this does not do.** The reviewed share catches drift in the
grader; it does not catch a failure mode the rubric does not name. The
production review of [chapter 8](./production.mdx) is where those
appear.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Validation:** the script in this chapter; a hundred items per task; readers' own agreement measured.
- **Bias checks:** length, position, verbosity, self-preference where the grader and the graded system share a model; randomized order.
- **Prompting:** per-question structured output with reasons; rules against the measured biases; versioned.
- **Operation:** a reviewed share per run; re-validation on change; disagreement between graders as a review signal.

## Common mistakes

- **A grader trusted because it is consistent.** Consistent with itself; validate against people.
- **One overall score.** Per-question grades with reasons are what readers can check.
- **Validation done once.** The model behind the grader changes; validate again.
- **Averaging two graders.** Their disagreement is the signal; use it for review.

## Verification

- **Record:** every grader in use has a validation with agreement, kappa, false passes, and bias checks, with the grader's version.
- **Rubric:** contains the rules the checks required; order randomized.
- **Operation:** each run has a reviewed share with its agreement; re-validation ran after the last change.
