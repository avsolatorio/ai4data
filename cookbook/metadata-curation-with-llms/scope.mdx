# 1. Scope of model assistance

**Question:** Which curation tasks can a model assist, and which stay with people?

## Rationale

Curation tasks differ in what a wrong output costs. A model that drafts a
keyword list costs a curator a minute to correct; a model that rewrites a
definition can change the meaning of a series for every future user. The
first decision is therefore which tasks a model may take, in which role
(draft, flag, align, translate, summarize), and which decisions stay with
a person. The second is what may be sent to a model at all: a record of a
public series is public; an unpublished record or a confidential
microdata dictionary is not.

## Target state

- A typology lists the curation tasks, the role a model takes in each, and the decision that remains with a curator.
- A rule states that nothing reaches the published catalog without a named curator's approval, and that confidential or unpublished records are never sent to external services.
- The records to start with are chosen from use and from completeness, and the team knows what success looks like for them.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Write the typology and the two rules. Choose twenty records to start with. |
| **AI-ready** | Add decision rights per field (which fields a model may draft, which it may only flag). Record the model, provider, and data sensitivity in the AI component register. |
| **AI-native** | Review the scope each period from acceptance rates and incidents, and extend or restrict the tasks accordingly. |

:::info[Running example]
The example organization allows a model to draft definitions and keywords,
to flag inconsistencies in any field, and to propose vocabulary mappings,
for its five public indicator records. Units and methodology text may be
flagged but never drafted. Every suggestion goes to one curator, who
records a decision.
:::

## Recipes

<Recipe title="1.1 Write the task typology with decision rights" level="Foundational" skills="Head of the curation unit" time="Half a day">

**Result:** a one-page table that says, for each curation task, what the
model does and what the curator decides.

| Task | Model role | Curator decides |
|---|---|---|
| Missing description or definition | Draft from the record and the schema | Accept, edit, or reject the draft |
| Keywords and topics | Draft; map free text to the vocabulary | Confirm the mapping |
| Name against definition, unit against values | Flag with a severity and a proposed fix | Whether the flag is right and what the fix is |
| Methodology and limitations text | Flag only | Any change |
| Translations of labels | Draft, marked machine-translated | Native-speaker review before publication |
| Summaries for catalog pages | Draft | Accept or edit |

1. Start from the table and adapt it to the organization's fields and
   risks; the program's [metadata-quality work](/docs/metadata-quality/generative-ai-for-metadata-quality)
   names the same task families: field generation, semantic alignment,
   vocabulary standardization, translation, summarization, and diagnostics.
2. Mark which fields a model may never draft (units, methods, legal text)
   and which it may only flag.
3. Name the curator role that decides, and the record of decisions
   ([chapter 5](./review.mdx), recipe 4.3).

**What this does not do.** The typology sets roles; it does not make
drafts good. [Chapters 2](./draft.mdx) and [3](./assess.mdx) measure
them.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="1.2 Set the confidentiality and approval rules" level="Foundational" skills="Head of the curation unit; legal" time="One day">

**Result:** two rules every staff member and every pipeline follows.

1. Confidentiality: only public records, or records cleared for
   publication, are sent to a model. Unpublished records and microdata
   dictionaries go only to models the organization runs itself. Record
   the rule in the AI-use policy of the
   [dissemination cookbook](/cookbook/ai-ready-dissemination/govern).
2. Approval: a model's output reaches the published catalog only after a
   named curator records a decision. Model output is marked as such until
   then (a `draft` status, or a note field).
3. Register the pipeline (model, provider, data used, owner, review date)
   in the AI component register; the program's tools are provider-agnostic
   (through litellm), so the model can be a local one.

**What this does not do.** Rules state the practice; the review board and
the decision log of [chapter 5](./review.mdx) are where the practice is
visible and auditable.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="1.3 Choose the records to start with" level="Foundational" skills="Curator; dissemination officer" time="Two hours">

**Result:** a first set of records where model assistance is worth the
review effort, and a definition of success for them.

1. Take the records with the highest use ([monitoring of data use](/cookbook/monitoring-data-use/)) and the lowest completeness (the
   [completeness check](/cookbook/ai-ready-dissemination/find) at the
   AI-ready level).
2. Start with twenty: enough to measure acceptance, few enough to review
   every suggestion.
3. Define success: the share of drafts accepted or edited, the share of
   flags the curator confirms, and the completeness gain after the
   decisions are applied.
4. Record the baseline (completeness, curator time per record) before the
   first run.

**What this does not do.** Twenty records calibrate the workflow. The
catalog-scale run comes after the acceptance rates of [chapter 8](./improve.mdx)
are known.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Typology:** the task families of the program's metadata-quality work; roles draft, flag, align, translate, summarize.
- **Rules:** the AI-use policy template and the AI component register of the dissemination cookbook.
- **Models:** hosted or local through a provider-agnostic client (litellm, as the program's tools use); local models for anything unpublished.
- **Record selection:** use counts from monitoring and completeness from the profile check.

## Common mistakes

- **Scope by tool.** The question is which tasks, in which role; the tool follows.
- **Pasting unpublished records into a public assistant.** The first rule exists for this.
- **Drafting the method text.** A model that rewrites the methodology changes the series' meaning; flag only.
- **Starting with the whole catalog.** Review capacity decides the scale; twenty records first.

## Verification

- **Typology:** every curation task in the team's procedures has a row with a role and a decision.
- **Rules:** staff can state both; the register has the pipeline with an owner.
- **Start set:** twenty records chosen with recorded use and completeness, and a baseline.
