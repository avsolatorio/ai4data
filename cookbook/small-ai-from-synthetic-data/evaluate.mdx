# 5. Evaluation on real text

**Question:** How well does the model do on real documents?

## Rationale

A model trained on generated sentences has learned how the generating
model writes about each topic. Real documents are written differently.
Their sentences are longer, they mix topics, and they use the vocabulary of their field. The only way to know
how the small model does on the documents of the collection is to
measure it on real documents whose topics were assigned by someone
other than the model or its generator.

Those reference labels can come from two sources. The best is a set of
documents labelled by staff for this purpose, with two people labelling
each document and their disagreements resolved. Where an existing
catalogue already assigns subjects to documents, its subjects can serve
as a reference after a crosswalk maps them to the labels. A catalogue's
subjects were assigned for another purpose, so they are incomplete for
some labels, and the evaluation reports that limitation with its scores.

The evaluation also answers the question of [chapter 1](./task.mdx):
whether the small model is good enough. The answer comes from a
comparison on the same documents with a large language model that labels
each document from instructions alone. The comparison gives the
difference in quality and the difference in cost, and the task owner
decides whether the small model's quality is enough for the use.

## Target state

- A real test set exists, with reference labels assigned independently of the model, and its source and limits are documented.
- The decision threshold for documents is chosen on a calibration part of the real documents, separate from the documents used to report the score.
- The small model and a large model are scored on the same documents with micro and macro F1, and the per-label scores are read.
- The task owner has decided, from the scores and the costs, whether the small model is used and for what.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Build a real test set from existing catalogue subjects or from 200 documents labelled by staff, and report micro and macro F1. |
| **AI-ready** | Calibrate the threshold on a separate part of the real documents, compare with a large model on the same documents, and keep the scores with the model version. |
| **AI-native** | Label a real test set with two annotators per document, measure their agreement, and run the evaluation automatically for every new model and every new round of generated data. |

:::info[Running example]
The chapter builds a test set of 1,792 abstracts of World Bank Policy
Research Working Papers published from 2016 to 2020, with reference
labels from the subjects of the Documents and Reports catalogue. It
calibrates the threshold on 1,492 abstracts and reports the scores of the
small model and of gpt-oss-120b on the other 300.
:::

## Recipes

<Recipe title="5.1 Build a real test set with independent labels" level="Foundational" skills="Data scientist; task owner" time="One to three days">

**Result:** a file of real documents with reference labels, built by a
script from a documented source, and a crosswalk that records how the
source's subjects map to the labels.

A crosswalk is a table that maps the subjects of one classification to
the labels of another. Only clear correspondences go into it. A subject
that is broader than any label, or that mixes two labels, is left out,
because a doubtful mapping would count correct predictions as errors.
The script `build_real_testset.py` downloads documents from the World
Bank Documents and Reports API, maps their subjects with the crosswalk,
and keeps the documents that have an abstract of at least 40 words and
at least one mapped label.

The crosswalk has two columns, `source_topic` and `label`. One subject
can map to two labels, as "Water Supply and Sanitation" does to Water
Supply and to Sanitation.

```python file=../../website/static/cookbook-files/small-ai-from-synthetic-data/build_real_testset.py
```

1. Choose a source of real documents like the ones in the collection,
   and a source of reference labels that is independent of the model.
2. Write the crosswalk with the task owner. Keep only correspondences
   that a curator would accept without discussion.
3. Run the script and read which labels the test set can measure. A
   label that never appears in the reference cannot be evaluated.
4. Where the catalogue subjects are not available, label 200 documents
   with two members of staff each, and use those labels.

**Worked example.** The crosswalk of the example has 71 rows and maps
catalogue subjects to 45 of the 86 labels. Subjects that the catalogue
adds to whole groups of documents together, such as "Disability",
which appears with "Access of Poor to Social Services" on papers about
cash transfers, were left out after a sample of the documents was read.

```text
$ python build_real_testset.py crosswalk.csv real_test.jsonl
papers downloaded   1922
papers kept         1792
labels measured     45
most frequent: Macroeconomics (700), Labor Markets (565), Poverty (492), Trade (460), Education (449)
least frequent: Indigenous Peoples (10), Biodiversity (6), Girls' Education (2)
wrote real_test.jsonl
```

The file [real_sample.jsonl](pathname:///cookbook-files/small-ai-from-synthetic-data/real_sample.jsonl)
holds twelve of these abstracts with their labels and their catalogue
subjects.

**What this does not do.** Catalogue subjects describe a document as a
whole and were assigned for search, so a paper can be about a topic its
subjects do not name. A predicted label missing from the reference is
then counted as an error although it may be right. The scores in this
chapter are therefore lower bounds on precision.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="5.2 Calibrate the threshold on part of the real documents" level="AI-ready" skills="Data scientist" time="Two hours">

**Result:** the threshold for document labels is chosen on a
calibration part of the real test set, and the score is reported on the
remaining documents.

The threshold chosen in training applies to single generated sentences.
A document is scored by combining the scores of its sentences
([chapter 6](./apply.mdx)), and the combined scores are on a different
scale. An average over ten sentences, of which two are about a topic,
is much lower than the score of either of those two sentences. The
threshold for documents is therefore chosen again, on real documents
that are not used to report the score.

1. Split the real test set into a calibration part and a reporting part.
   The example keeps 300 documents for reporting and calibrates on the
   other 1,492.
2. Score the calibration documents with `predict.py` and run
   `evaluate.py --sweep` with the limit on the number of labels the use
   requires.
3. Choose the threshold with the highest micro F1, and report the score
   on the reporting documents with that threshold.

**Worked example.** The documents are scored by the average of their
sentence scores, and at most five labels are assigned to each.

```text
$ python evaluate.py real_calib.jsonl scores.jsonl --sweep --top 5
threshold 0.02   micro F1 0.395
threshold 0.03   micro F1 0.393
threshold 0.05   micro F1 0.387
threshold 0.075  micro F1 0.383
threshold 0.1    micro F1 0.369
threshold 0.15   micro F1 0.331
threshold 0.2    micro F1 0.291
threshold 0.3    micro F1 0.223
threshold 0.4    micro F1 0.168
threshold 0.5    micro F1 0.119
```

The best threshold for documents is 0.02, against 0.3 for sentences in
training. At 0.3, the document score would be 0.223.

**What this does not do.** One threshold serves all labels. Thresholds
per label fit better, but they need enough calibration documents for
each label. In the example, 31 of the 45 measured labels have at least 30 calibration documents.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="5.3 Compare with a large model on the same documents" level="AI-ready" skills="Data scientist" time="Half a day">

**Result:** the small model and a large language model have been scored
on the same reporting documents, with the cost and time per document of
each, and the task owner has the figures to decide.

The script `llm_baseline.py` sends each document with the full label
list to a large model and asks for up to five labels in a structure that
only admits label names. It writes the answers in the format of
`predict.py`, with a score of 1 for each chosen label, so that the same
evaluation applies. The large model receives no examples and no
training. The comparison therefore shows what the organization would
get by calling a large model for every document.

```python file=../../website/static/cookbook-files/small-ai-from-synthetic-data/llm_baseline.py
```

1. Run the large model on the reporting documents, with the same limit
   of labels per document as the small model.
2. Evaluate both with `evaluate.py`, the small model at the calibrated
   threshold and the large model at 0.5.
3. Put the scores beside the cost and time per document, and give the
   table to the task owner.

**Worked example.** On the 300 reporting abstracts, with at most five
labels per document, the scores are as follows. The first small model is
the one trained on the public dataset in [chapter 4](./train.mdx). The
second was trained on the set generated with topic names allowed, which
did best in the comparison of recipe 3.3.

| | Small model, public dataset | Small model, names allowed | gpt-oss-120b |
|---|---|---|---|
| Micro precision | 0.431 | 0.486 | 0.506 |
| Micro recall | 0.421 | 0.436 | 0.515 |
| Micro F1 | 0.426 | 0.460 | 0.511 |
| Macro F1 | 0.354 | 0.380 | 0.421 |
| Share of cells right | 0.909 | 0.918 | 0.921 |
| Tokens per document | none | none | 741 in, 228 out |
| Seconds per document | 0.007 on a GPU, 0.17 on four CPU cores | the same | 5.4 per request |

The large model scores higher by 0.085 in micro F1 than the first small
model, and by 0.051 than the second. All three are weakest on Water
Resources Management, at 0.10, which points to the reference rather
than the models. The catalogue subject "Water Resources", which the
crosswalk maps to that label, comes with "Hydrology" on 105 of its 141
papers, so it is likely added with a broader group of subjects. All
three score well on Disaster Risk Management, Teachers, and Labor
Markets. [Chapter 6](./apply.mdx) turns the token counts into costs for
the collection.

**What this does not do.** The large model was given the label names
only. Definitions and examples in its prompt would likely raise its
score, at the cost of more tokens per document. The small model was
trained on one generated set with one set of settings; the comparison
of generated sets in recipe 3.3 is the first way to raise its score.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- Reference labels from staff are the strongest evidence. An annotation tool such as Label Studio records the labels of two annotators and their agreement.
- Where the organization's catalogue uses a controlled vocabulary, such as a thesaurus of subjects, the crosswalk can be published with the label set as SKOS mappings (`skos:exactMatch`, `skos:closeMatch`), and only exact matches are used for evaluation.
- The evaluation scripts read any file with an `id` and `labels`, so the same evaluation applies to sentences, paragraphs, or whole documents.

## World Bank examples

- The [Documents and Reports API](https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/api) provides the abstracts and the catalogue subjects of the running example.
- The [evaluation suites cookbook](/cookbook/evaluation-suites/) describes how a test set becomes a suite that is run for every model release.

## Resources

- [scikit-learn multilabel metrics](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/model_evaluation.html#multilabel-ranking-metrics) defines micro and macro averaging as computed by `evaluate.py`.
- [SKOS mapping properties](https://www.w3.org/TR/skos-reference/#mapping) define exact and close matches for publishing a crosswalk.
- [Label Studio](https://labelstud.io/) records labels from several annotators for a staff-labelled test set.

## Common mistakes

- **Reporting only the score on generated test sentences.** The first model of the example scores 0.685 on generated test sentences and 0.426 on real abstracts.
- **Using the training threshold for documents.** Combined document scores are on another scale, and the training threshold of 0.3 lowers the document score from 0.395 to 0.223 on the calibration documents.
- **Calibrating and reporting on the same documents.** The reported score then includes a choice fitted to those documents.
- **Reading a low score as a model failure without reading the reference.** The weakest label of both models in the example is a crosswalk problem.

## Verification

- The real test set is rebuilt by the script from the crosswalk, and its counts match the ones recorded.
- No reporting document was used to choose the threshold.
- Both models were scored on the same documents with the same limit on labels per document.
