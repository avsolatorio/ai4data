# 7. Release, licensing, and maintenance

**Question:** How are the model and its data documented, licensed, and kept current?

## Rationale

A small model trained on generated data has two parents: the pretrained
encoder and the large model that wrote the examples. Each comes with
terms. A user of the model, inside or outside the organization, needs
to know both, along with what the model was trained to do, how it
scored on real documents, and where it fails. A dataset card and a
model card are the standard places for that information. Model hubs
display them with the files, and other organizations look for them
before they reuse a model.

The generated data deserve a card of their own. A reader of the
examples cannot tell that a large model wrote them, which model it was,
or that some sentences invent figures and attribute them to real
institutions. The card states these facts, so that nobody mistakes a
generated sentence for a quotation from a real report.

A topic classifier also ages. The label list changes when the
organization revises its topics, new kinds of documents enter the
collection, and better generating and pretrained models appear. The
model is therefore retrained on a rule: when the labels change, when the
evaluation on newly sampled real documents falls, or when a new
generator or encoder improves the score in the comparison of
[chapter 5](./evaluate.mdx).

## Target state

- The generated data have a dataset card that names the generating model, its terms, the prompt version, the request plan, the check report, and the known issues.
- The model has a model card that names the encoder and its licence, the training data, the labels, the threshold, the scores on real documents, the intended use, and the known errors.
- The licence of the model and of the data has been confirmed against the terms of every model involved.
- A retraining rule is written, and each release keeps its label file, data, model, and evaluation together under one version.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Write a dataset card and a model card from the templates, with the generating model, the scores on real documents, and the known errors. |
| **AI-ready** | Publish the model and the data with their cards and licences on a model hub or in the organization's catalogue, versioned with the label file and the evaluation. |
| **AI-native** | Retrain on a written rule, run the evaluation of chapter 5 for every release automatically, and publish the scores with each version. |

## Recipes

<Recipe title="7.1 Write the dataset card for the generated examples" level="Foundational" skills="Data scientist" time="Half a day">

**Result:** the generated examples have a dataset card from which a reader can judge their origin and the uses they suit.

A dataset card is a short document published with a dataset. The
template [dataset_card.md](pathname:///cookbook-files/small-ai-from-synthetic-data/dataset_card.md)
has the sections that matter for generated data: the generating model
and its terms, the prompt and the request plan, the counts from the
check report, the fields of the files, the intended use, and the known
issues. Its front matter follows the metadata format of the Hugging Face
Hub, so the card can be published there without changes.

1. Copy the template and fill each section from the records of chapters
   2 and 3: the model identifier, the prompt version, the plan, the
   check report, and the cost.
2. State plainly that every sentence was written by a language model and
   that names, places, and figures in the sentences are invented.
3. Give the licence of the data, and the conditions of the generating
   model's terms that apply to them.
4. Publish the card with the raw replies and the cleaned examples.

**Worked example.** The card of the example's generated set names
gpt-oss-120b under the Apache 2.0 licence and the prompt version, and it
reports the counts of chapter 3. Under known issues, it records that
some sentences attribute invented figures to real institutions, such as
"The 2025 World Bank report on debt relief recommends extending grace
periods for low-income countries by up to five years."

**What this does not do.** The card describes the data. It does not
make them fit for a use that their generating model's terms exclude.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="7.2 Write the model card" level="Foundational" skills="Data scientist; task owner" time="Half a day">

**Result:** the model has a model card that states its task, its scores on real documents, and its known errors.

A model card is the model's equivalent of a dataset card. The template
[model_card.md](pathname:///cookbook-files/small-ai-from-synthetic-data/model_card.md)
has sections for the intended use, the training data, the training
procedure, the evaluation, the known errors, and the licences. The
evaluation section reports the scores on real documents with their
source, and the scores on generated test sentences only beside them, as
an upper bound.

1. Copy the template and fill it from `classifier.json`, the training
   log, and the evaluation of chapter 5.
2. Write the intended use as the task statement of recipe 1.1 says it:
   suggestions to curators, for example, with no automatic publication.
3. List the known errors with figures, such as labels that the model
   over-assigns, and the labels the evaluation could not measure.
4. State the licence of the model and the terms inherited from the
   encoder and from the generating model, as confirmed by the legal
   office (recipe 2.1).

**Worked example.** The [model card of the example](pathname:///cookbook-files/small-ai-from-synthetic-data/model_card.md)
describes the classifier trained on the set generated with topic names
allowed. Its evaluation table gives the micro F1 of 0.460 on the 300
reporting abstracts beside the 0.758 on generated test sentences, and
the 0.511 of gpt-oss-120b on the same abstracts. Its known errors list
the three most over-assigned labels with their counts, the weakest
labels, and the 42 labels that the reference does not measure. Its
licence section states that the generating model's licence places no
conditions on its outputs.

**What this does not do.** A card states what was measured. A use
outside the intended use, such as counting documents by topic for a
published statistic, needs its own evaluation.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="7.3 Retrain on a written rule" level="AI-ready" skills="Data scientist; task owner" time="One day to write; one to two days per release">

**Result:** a written rule says when the model is retrained, and each
release keeps the label file, the generated data, the model, and its
evaluation together under one version.

A rule replaces ad hoc retraining. It names the triggers and the checks
that a new model must pass before it replaces the current one. Each
release is a folder or a tagged repository state that holds every input
needed to rebuild the model, so that a question about a past suggestion
can be answered from the version that made it.

1. Write the triggers. Typical triggers are a change to the label file,
   a fall in micro F1 of more than a set amount on a fresh sample of real
   documents, and a new generator or encoder that the comparison of
   chapter 5 shows to be better.
2. Write the acceptance check. A new model replaces the current one only
   when its micro and macro F1 on the reporting documents are at least
   as high, and the frequency check of recipe 6.3 shows no new
   over-assigned label.
3. Version each release with the label file, the plan, the raw replies,
   the cleaned examples, the model folder, and the evaluation output.
4. Keep the previous release available until the new one has been used
   by the curators for a full cycle.

**Worked example.** The rule for the topic classifier names three
triggers:

- a revision of the topic list on the World Bank's topic page;
- a fall of more than 0.03 in micro F1 on 200 newly sampled working papers, checked each quarter;
- a new open-weight generator or encoder.

The acceptance check compares the new model with the current one on the 300
reporting abstracts of chapter 5. The threshold of 0.03 is larger than
the differences of up to 0.027 in micro F1 that the tests for this
guide measured between training runs that differed only in their random
seed.

**What this does not do.** The rule detects a fall in quality on
documents like the reference. A new kind of document with no reference
labels is found only when curators report bad suggestions, which the
AI-native level records.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- The [Hugging Face Hub](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/model-cards) displays model and dataset cards with the files and supports private repositories for review before publication.
- An organization that publishes through its own catalogue can describe the model and the data with the metadata standards it already uses, and link to the cards.
- [Croissant](https://docs.mlcommons.org/croissant/) describes a machine-learning dataset in a machine-readable form, which the [ML-ready datasets cookbook](/cookbook/ml-ready-datasets/) covers.

## World Bank examples

- The [ai4data organization on the Hugging Face Hub](https://huggingface.co/ai4data) publishes the program's models, including the [GISTEmbed models](https://huggingface.co/collections/ai4data/gistembed), with their cards.
- The [Small and Open Models cookbook](/cookbook/small-and-open-models/) covers model cards, provenance, and the security of model files.

## Resources

- [Hugging Face model cards](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/model-cards) and [dataset cards](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/datasets-cards) give the metadata format of the templates.
- [Model Cards for Model Reporting](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993) is the paper that defined the model card.
- [Datasheets for Datasets](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09010) is the paper that defined the questions a dataset card answers.

## Common mistakes

- **Publishing generated sentences without saying so.** A reader can take an invented figure attributed to a real institution as a quotation.
- **Reporting only the synthetic test score in the card.** It overstated the score on real abstracts by about a quarter of the scale in the example.
- **Leaving the generator out of the model card.** The terms of the generating model can apply to the model, and a user cannot check them without its name.
- **Retraining whenever a new model appears.** Without an acceptance check, a new release can score lower than the release before it.

## Verification

- Both cards name the generating model with its version, and the model card names the encoder with its licence.
- The model card reports scores on real documents with their source and date.
- The release folder rebuilds the model and reproduces its evaluation.
