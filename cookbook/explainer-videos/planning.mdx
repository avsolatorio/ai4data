# 2. Facts and the scene outline

**Question:** How is a video planned from its source?

## Rationale

An explainer video states facts in the organization's name, in the
organization's voice, to readers who will not check them. Every figure,
definition, and date in it has to come from a published source, and the
team has to be able to show which source. When an AI agent drafts the
narration, this requirement becomes more pressing: a language model writes
fluent sentences about labour statistics from its training data, and a
figure or a definition from that memory can differ from the
organization's own. The fact sheet is the list of what the video may
state, and the agent works from it.

The order of the scenes is the second decision. A viewer follows a video
in one pass, so the video has to set out the context before the problem
and the problem before the method. The program's explainers use one
sequence of scenes for every topic, which makes each video easier to
plan, and which lets a viewer who has seen one video find the parts of
the next. The outline is the plan of that sequence for one topic, one
line per scene.

## Target state

- Every fact the video will state is in a fact sheet with its source and the place in the source.
- The scene outline follows the standard sequence, with one line per scene saying what is shown and what the narration says.
- The owner of the source has read the fact sheet and the outline before any narration is written.
- The AI agent that drafts the narration receives the fact sheet as its only source of figures.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Write the fact sheet of recipe 2.1 and the outline of recipe 2.2 by hand, and have the owner of the source read the two documents. |
| **AI-ready** | Let the agent draft the fact sheet from the source documents with recipe 2.3, check each row against the source, and keep the fact sheet with the video's files. |
| **AI-native** | Generate the figure rows of the fact sheet from the release table or the data API, so that a revision of the source updates the fact sheet. |

:::info[Running example]
The team writes a fact sheet of eleven rows for the release video: the
four headline figures with their previous values, the provisional status
of the unemployment rate, and the definitions of employment, unemployment,
the labour force, and the two rates. The figures come from
[release_table.csv](pathname:///cookbook-files/explainer-videos/release_table.csv),
and the definitions from the methodology of the survey, which follows the
international standard. The outline has eleven scenes in the standard order,
and the head of the labour statistics unit reads both before the
narration is written.
:::

## Recipes

<Recipe title="2.1 Write a fact sheet with a source for every fact" level="Foundational" skills="Dissemination officer" time="Two to three hours">

**Result:** the team has a table of every figure, definition, date, and
claim the video will state, each with its source, the place in the
source, and the person who checked it.

A fact sheet separates the research from the writing. The person who
writes it reads the source documents once, with care, and records each
fact that the video may use. The narration of chapter 3 then uses only
facts from the sheet, and the review of chapter 6 checks the narration
against the sheet and the sheet against the source. Figures come from the
values recorded in the published source. Where a figure has to be
recalculated, the calculation is run in an environment built from the
source's own requirements and the result is recorded with the calculation.

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Fact | The statement as the video will make it, in a full sentence. |
| Value | The figure, with its unit and reference period, where the fact has one. |
| Source | The identifier of the dataset or document. |
| Place | The table row, the paragraph, or the page where the fact appears. |
| Checked by | The role of the person who compared the row with the source. |

1. List the sources: the release table, the report, and the methodology
   note. Use the published versions, because the video will be compared
   with them.
2. Write one row per fact. A definition is a fact; write it in the words
   of the methodology and note the paragraph.
3. Mark the status of each figure where the source gives one (provisional,
   revised, final), because the narration has to say it.
4. Leave out any fact that has no source. A fact that the team believes
   but cannot cite does not go into the video.

**Worked example.** Four rows of the team's fact sheet read as follows.

| Fact | Value | Source | Place | Checked by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The unemployment rate in the second quarter of 2025 was 6.1 percent, after 6.3 percent in the first quarter. | 6.1; 6.3 | `EX-LFS-2025-Q2` release table | Row `LF_UNEMP_PCT` | Dissemination officer |
| The second-quarter unemployment rate is provisional. | P | `EX-LFS-2025-Q2` release table | Column `status` | Dissemination officer |
| Youth unemployment, for people aged 15 to 24, was 16.8 percent, after 17.5 percent. | 16.8; 17.5 | `EX-LFS-2025-Q2` release table | Row `LF_UNEMP_PCT_YOUTH` | Dissemination officer |
| A person counts as unemployed when without work, having looked for work in the last four weeks, and able to start now. | | Survey methodology, after the ILO resolution | Paragraph 47 of the resolution | Labour statistics unit |

**What this does not do.** The fact sheet records what the sources say.
It does not decide which facts the video needs; the outline of recipe 2.2
does that, and facts the outline does not use stay on the sheet for a
later video.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="2.2 Write the scene outline in the standard order" level="Foundational" skills="Dissemination officer" time="Two hours">

**Result:** the team has an outline of the video as a list of scenes in
the standard order, one line per scene, with the facts from the sheet
that each scene uses.

A scene is one screen of the video with its own heading, for example the
definition of unemployment. A scene has one or more beats: each beat is
one change on screen with one or two sentences of narration. The standard
order below comes from the program's production guide, where it was set
by review of the first explainer and followed by the second. A data release fits the same order as a method. The context is the
survey and the release, and the problem is the misreading of a figure.
The method steps are the definitions and the calculation, and the result
is the release as a user reads it.

| Scene | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Title | Name the topic and say in one sentence what the video explains. |
| Context | Show where the topic sits, as a flow of a few elements. |
| Context example | Show one concrete case, animated. |
| The problem | Show what goes wrong without the method or the definition. |
| Method steps | One scene per step, starting from the inputs. |
| The result for the user | Show what a user sees when the method is applied. |
| Scope and the human judgement | Say what the method or the figures cover, what remains a judgement, and who makes it. |
| Closing | Give the addresses of the sources. |

1. Write one line per scene: the heading, what appears and moves on
   screen, and the rows of the fact sheet it uses.
2. Keep the method steps to the number the method has. A release with a
   classification, a calculation, and a status has three or four steps;
   do not add steps to fill time.
3. End the argument on the human role. A video that explains a figure
   also says what the figure does not say and who interprets it.
4. Estimate the length: three to five minutes holds about eight to twelve
   scenes at two to four beats each.

**Worked example.** The team's outline has eleven scenes, which the step
script of chapter 3 numbers as the program's stages do.

- `s1`, title: the name of the release and one sentence on the content of the video.
- `s2`, context: the survey and the release as a flow from households to the release table.
- `s2b`, context example: a journalist reads the release and looks for the unemployment rate.
- `s3`, the problem: three misreadings of the 6.1 percent.
- `s4` to `s7`, method steps: the classification into employed, unemployed, and outside the labour force; the definition of unemployment; the two rates as divisions; provisional figures.
- `s8`, the result for the user: the four headline figures.
- `s9`, scope and the human judgement: what the release covers and the role of the analysts.
- `s10`, closing: the address of the release.

**What this does not do.** The outline fixes the order and the content of
the scenes. It does not fix their design; the stage page of chapter 4
does that, under the organization's production guide.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="2.3 Draft the fact sheet with an AI agent and check each row" level="AI-ready" skills="Dissemination officer with an AI agent" time="An hour, plus the check">

**Result:** the agent has drafted the fact sheet from the source files,
and a person has compared every row with the source and corrected or
removed the rows that do not match.

An AI agent with access to the source files can read a release table and
a methodology note faster than a person and write the rows of the fact
sheet with their places. The agent can also misread a table, take a
previous value for a current one, or supply a definition from its
training data. The person's check is therefore part of the recipe, and
the agent's draft is never used before it.

1. Give the agent the source files and the column table of recipe 2.1,
   with the instruction to write only facts that appear in the files and
   to give the place of each.
2. Check every row against the source: the value, the period, the status,
   and the place. Mark the row with the role of the person who checked it.
3. Remove rows whose place cannot be found. Record a pattern of errors
   (for example previous values taken as current) in the production guide
   of chapter 4, so that the instruction can be improved.

**Worked example.** The agent drafted twelve rows from the release table
and the methodology note. The dissemination officer found two problems.
One row gave the participation rate as 60.9 percent for the second
quarter, which is the first-quarter value in the column
`previous_value`. A second row defined the labour force participation rate
with an age limit of 15 to 64, which is not in the organization's
methodology; the release table gives the rate for the population aged 15
and over. The officer corrected the first row and removed the second, and
the sheet went to the owner with eleven rows checked.

**What this does not do.** The check compares the rows with the source
files that the agent read. A fact that the agent left out is found only
when the person reads the source against the outline.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Fact sheet format.** A spreadsheet, a table in the content system, or a CSV file kept with the video's files. A CSV file can be read by a script that checks the narration against it, as the release table is read in chapter 3.
- **Sources for the figures.** The published release table, the organization's data API, or the saved output of a notebook. The program's guide prefers values recorded in the source to values computed again.
- **Outline as a document or as the first draft of the step script.** A short team can write the outline directly as the list of scenes in the step script of chapter 3, with one placeholder line per beat.

## Resources

- [Resolution concerning statistics of work, employment and labour underutilization](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/wcmsp5/groups/public/@dgreports/@stat/documents/normativeinstrument/wcms_230304.pdf): the ILO standard, as amended by the 21st International Conference of Labour Statisticians in 2023, whose paragraphs 27 and 47 define employment and unemployment for the fact sheet.
- [Release table](pathname:///cookbook-files/explainer-videos/release_table.csv): the source of the figure rows of the running example's fact sheet.

## Common mistakes

- **Facts from memory.** A definition that the team or the agent knows without a source can differ from the organization's methodology, as the age limit of 15 to 64 did in the running example.
- **A previous value taken as the current value.** A release table puts the two side by side, and an agent or a tired reader takes the wrong column.
- **The status left out.** A provisional figure stated without the word "provisional" is quoted as final.
- **Method steps added for length.** A step that the method does not have confuses the viewer about the method.

## Verification

- **Source check.** A second person picks three rows of the fact sheet at random and finds each fact at its place in the source.
- **Coverage check.** Every fact that the outline uses has a row in the fact sheet.
- **Owner check.** The owner of the source has read the fact sheet and the outline and recorded the date of the reading.
