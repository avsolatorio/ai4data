# 7. Publishing and upkeep

**Question:** How is a video published, catalogued, and kept current?

## Rationale

An explainer serves the reader of a release best when the video sits beside the figures under discussion. A
video on a platform channel alone is found by the platform's visitors,
and a reader of the release page does not see it. The video therefore
sits on the pages that discuss its topic, with its transcript, its
sources, and the disclosure of its voice, and it is described in the
organization's catalogue like any other product.

A catalogue record makes the video findable by software. A record in the
schema.org vocabulary, embedded in the page, tells search engines and
catalogues what the video is, how long it lasts, which data it is based
on, under which licence it is published, and that it has captions and a
transcript. The same record links the video to its sources by their
identifiers, which is what makes upkeep possible.

A video states figures of a given release. When the release is revised or
a new one is published, the video either stays valid for its reference
period or states something that is no longer true. The organization
needs to know which of its videos a release affects, and it needs a
procedure to remake or withdraw them.

## Target state

- Each video is hosted where the organization controls its availability, with the master file and the captions kept in the organization's archive.
- Each video is embedded on the pages that discuss its topic, with its transcript, its sources, and its disclosure.
- Each video has a catalogue record with its duration, its sources, its captions, its licence, and its narration disclosure.
- Each release or revision is checked against the videos that use its data, and the affected videos are remade or withdrawn.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Host the video with recipe 7.1 and embed it on the release page with recipe 7.2. |
| **AI-ready** | Write the catalogue record of recipe 7.3 for every video and embed it in the page, and follow the upkeep of recipe 7.4 at each release. |
| **AI-native** | Generate the catalogue record from the video card at each recording, and let the release process list the videos whose sources it changes. |

:::info[Running example]
The team publishes the release video on the organization's own site,
embeds it on the release page and on the methodology page of the survey,
and writes its catalogue record from
[video_card.json](pathname:///cookbook-files/explainer-videos/video_card.json).
The record lists the survey round `EX-LFS-2025-Q2` and the report
`EX-REP-2025-02` as sources. When the final figures for the second
quarter are published, the record shows that the video depends on them,
and the team remakes it.
:::

## Recipes

<Recipe title="7.1 Host the video on the organization's site or with a privacy-enhanced embed" level="Foundational" skills="Web team with the dissemination officer" time="Half a day to set up, then minutes per video">

**Result:** the video plays from a host that the organization controls or
has chosen with its privacy terms in view, with the captions available in
the player.

There are two common hosting options. The organization's own website can
serve the MP4 file with the HTML `video` element, which takes a caption
file through a `track` element of kind `captions`. The organization keeps
full control of the file and of what the page records about its viewers.
A video platform provides streaming at several qualities and a large
audience, and its embed loads the platform's player on the organization's
page. YouTube offers a privacy-enhanced mode, used by changing the embed
address from `www.youtube.com` to `www.youtube-nocookie.com`. In that
mode, the platform states that views of the embedded video are not used
to personalize the viewer's browsing experience or the advertising shown
outside the organization's site.

1. Keep the master files (the MP4, the caption file, the step script, and
   the stage page) in the organization's archive, whatever the host.
2. On the organization's site, publish the video with the `video`
   element, the caption file, and no automatic start. WCAG 2.2 requires a
   way to pause or stop audio that plays automatically for more than three
   seconds (success criterion 1.4.2), and a video that does not start by
   itself avoids the question.
3. On a platform, upload the caption file with the video, and embed it in
   the privacy-enhanced mode where the platform offers one.
4. Check that the captions can be turned on in the player on a phone and
   on a desktop browser.

A page on the organization's site embeds the video as follows; the
addresses are those of the running example.

```html
<video controls preload="metadata" width="1280" height="720"
       poster="https://stats.example/videos/lfs-2025-q2.jpg">
  <source src="https://stats.example/videos/lfs-2025-q2.mp4" type="video/mp4">
  <track kind="captions" srclang="en" label="English"
         src="https://stats.example/videos/lfs-2025-q2.vtt" default>
</video>
```

**Worked example.** The team hosts the release video on the organization's
site with the element above, and keeps the files in the archive folder of
the release. The organization's video channel carries a copy for its
subscribers, with the same caption file uploaded.

**What this does not do.** The recipe chooses a host. It does not assess
the platform's terms; the organization's data protection officer reads
them before the organization embeds a platform's player on its pages.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="7.2 Embed the video on the pages that discuss its topic" level="Foundational" skills="Dissemination officer" time="An hour per video">

**Result:** the video appears on each page where a reader meets its topic,
with the transcript, the sources, the date of the figures, and the
disclosure of the voice beside it.

A reader of the release page who sees a figure and does not know what it
means is the reader the video was made for. The video therefore goes
where that reader is: the release page, the methodology page, and the
glossary entry of the concept. Around the video, the page gives what the
video cannot: the transcript to search and quote, links to the sources,
the release whose figures the video states, and the line that says how
the narration was made.

1. List the pages that discuss the topic, from the site's search for the
   topic's terms.
2. Embed the video on each page, near the figures that the video explains.
3. Add below the video: the transcript (or a link to it), the sources
   with links, the sentence "The figures are those of the release of"
   with the release date, and the disclosure line of chapter 5.
4. Link the video from the catalogue record of recipe 7.3.

**Worked example.** The team embedded the release video on the release
page, under the release table, and on the methodology page of the survey,
beside the definitions. Under the video, each page shows the transcript
in an expandable section, the links to `EX-LFS-2025-Q2` and
`EX-REP-2025-02`, the line "The figures are those of the release of 20
August 2025", and the disclosure.

**What this does not do.** The recipe places the video on the
organization's own pages. Whether the video is shared on social media or
in newsletters is a decision of the communication plan, which this guide
does not cover.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="7.3 Write a catalogue record for each video" level="AI-ready" skills="Dissemination officer or metadata officer" time="Fifteen minutes per video">

**Result:** each video has a schema.org VideoObject record, embedded in
the page that hosts it and kept in the organization's catalogue.

schema.org is a vocabulary that search engines agree on for describing
things on web pages, and VideoObject is its type for a video. The record
is written in JSON-LD, which is JSON with the vocabulary attached, and it
is embedded in the page in a `script` element of type
`application/ld+json`. The script `video_record.py` writes the record
from a video card, a short JSON file that the team fills when the video
is approved. The card's fields map to the record as follows.

| Card field | Record property | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `title`, `description` | `name`, `description` | The title and a description of the content; the disclosure is added at the end of the description. |
| `duration_seconds` | `duration` | The length measured on the MP4 file, written as an ISO 8601 duration. |
| `sources` | `isBasedOn` | The datasets and documents the video uses, with their identifiers. |
| `captions`, `captions_url` | `caption`, `transcript` | The caption file, and the transcript taken from its text. |
| `license` | `license` | The licence under which the video is published. |
| `narration` | `digitalSourceType` | For a synthetic narration, `CompositeSyntheticDigitalSource`, the IPTC code for a composite of elements of which at least one is generated by AI. |

The record also lists the accessibility features `closedCaptions` and
`transcript`, and the access modes `auditory` and `visual`, from the
vocabulary that schema.org uses for these properties.

1. Fill the video card when the video is signed off. Measure the duration
   on the MP4 file, for example with `ffprobe`, and enter it.
2. Run the script. It fails when a required field is missing or when a
   synthetic narration has no disclosure that says "synthetic".
3. Embed the record in the page that hosts the video, and add it to the
   organization's catalogue.
4. Test the published page with the schema.org validator.

```bash
python video_record.py video_card.json --out video_record.jsonld
```

```text
wrote video_record.jsonld: VideoObject 'Labour Force Survey, second quarter 2025: headline figures and definitions'
duration PT3M34S, 2 sources, transcript of 526 words, narration synthetic
note: duration taken from the last caption cue; measure the MP4 and put it in the card
result: PASS
```

The first lines of the record are the following (abridged).

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "VideoObject",
  "identifier": "lfs-2025-q2-explainer",
  "name": "Labour Force Survey, second quarter 2025: headline figures and definitions",
  "inLanguage": "en",
  "duration": "PT3M34S",
  "uploadDate": "2025-08-20",
  "isBasedOn": [
    {"@type": "Dataset", "identifier": "EX-LFS-2025-Q2", "name": "Labour Force Survey, second quarter 2025", "url": "https://stats.example/data/EX-LFS-2025-Q2"},
    {"@type": "Report", "identifier": "EX-REP-2025-02", "name": "Labour market report 2025 Q2", "url": "https://stats.example/reports/EX-REP-2025-02"}
  ],
  "license": "https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/",
  "accessibilityFeature": ["closedCaptions", "transcript"],
  "digitalSourceType": "https://schema.org/CompositeSyntheticDigitalSource"
}
```

**Worked example.** The team ran the script on the card of the release
video. The card had no measured duration yet, and the script took the
duration from the last caption cue and said so. The video runs on for the
closing pause after the last cue, so the team measured the MP4, entered
the duration in the card, and ran the script again before publishing the
record.

**What this does not do.** The script writes the record from the card. It
does not check that the addresses resolve or that a particular search
engine shows the video; the validator and a search after publication
show that.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="7.4 Remake or withdraw a video when its data change" level="AI-ready" skills="Head of dissemination with the owner of the source" time="An hour per release, plus the remake">

**Result:** at each release and each revision, the team has listed the
videos whose sources it changes, and each affected video has been
remade, relabelled, or withdrawn.

A video states figures for a reference period. A new release for a later
period does not make it wrong, because it still explains the earlier
release correctly, and its title and its page say which release it
explains. A revision of the same period does make it wrong, and so does
the publication of final figures that the video calls provisional. The
identifiers in the catalogue record and the topic list of chapter 1 are
how the team finds the affected videos.

1. At each release and revision, search the catalogue records for the
   identifiers of the datasets the release changes.
2. For each video found, decide: keep it (a later period, with the title
   and page naming its release), remake it (a revision or a change of
   status), or withdraw it (a method change that the video no longer
   describes).
3. To remake, update the fact sheet, the step script, and the stage,
   run the checks of chapters 3 and 6, record, review, and sign off
   again. Publish the new version at the same address, and update the
   record with the new duration, a `dateModified`, and a `version`.
4. Keep the previous version in the archive with its sign-off record.

**Worked example.** When the office publishes the final figures for the
second quarter of 2025, the catalogue search for `EX-LFS-2025-Q2` returns
the release video. The video calls the unemployment rate of 6.1 percent
provisional, which is out of date once the final figure is published,
whatever its value. The team remakes the video with the final figures and
their status, signs it off again, and archives the first version.

**What this does not do.** The search finds the videos that list the
changed dataset as a source. A video that uses a figure without listing
its source is found only by reading; the fact sheet and the card of every
video list all sources for that reason.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Hosting.** The organization's own web server or content delivery network; a video platform with a privacy-enhanced embed; or both, with the organization's site as the address in the catalogue record.
- **Streaming formats.** For long or popular videos, an adaptive streaming format such as HLS or DASH serves the right quality to each device; a single MP4 file suits a five-minute explainer.
- **Catalogue.** The record can live in the organization's data catalogue next to the dataset records, or in the content system of the website, as long as it carries the source identifiers.
- **Metadata schema.** An organization that documents its products with the World Bank metadata schemas can describe a video with the schema for videos, which the Metadata Editor supports, and generate the schema.org record from it.

## Common mistakes

- **A video only on a platform channel.** The reader of the release page, who needs it, does not see it.
- **A record without sources.** The release process cannot find the video when its data change.
- **A duration taken from the captions.** The video runs on after the last caption, and the record understates its length.
- **A provisional figure left in a video after the final release.** The video tells viewers that a figure may change after it has been finalized.

## Verification

- **Embed check.** The video plays with captions on every page listed in recipe 7.2, on a phone and a desktop browser.
- **Record check.** The schema.org validator reads the record on the published page without errors, and the record lists every source of the fact sheet.
- **Upkeep check.** At the last release, the search of recipe 7.4 was run and its result recorded.
