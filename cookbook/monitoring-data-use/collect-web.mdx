# 5. Collection from policy, news, and web sources

**Question:** Can policy documents, news, and reports that use the data be collected?

## Rationale

The use that justifies a statistical organization is in policy: a
strategy that sets a target against an indicator, a budget speech that
quotes the unemployment rate, a parliamentary question that cites the
census, a newspaper that reports the release. None of it has a reference
list, and little of it is in a scholarly index. It sits on government
sites, in parliamentary records, in news archives, and in the reports of
international organizations. Collecting it means crawling, and crawling
has rules: the site's robots.txt, its terms of use, identification of
the crawler, and a rate the site can bear. An organization that collects
within those rules gets the evidence; one that does not risks its access
and its reputation.

## Target state

- The policy, news, and web sources in scope are listed with their terms, the collection method, and the licence of their content.
- Collection prefers exports, feeds, and APIs; crawling runs only where allowed, identified, and rate-limited, with a check against robots.txt before every fetch.
- Documents are stored with metadata and a text layer, deduplicated, and the coverage of known policy and media uses is measured.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | List the sources. Search the main government sites and news archives by hand each quarter for the canonical names; record the documents found. |
| **AI-ready** | Collect from document lists, feeds, and exports on a schedule; crawl within robots.txt with identification and rate limits; use news and web datasets for breadth; store metadata and text; measure coverage. |
| **AI-native** | Run collection automatically with the robots check as a gate; add sources from results; track coverage by source type over time. |

:::info[Running example]
Four of the six collected documents come from the web: two policy
documents from a ministry site, a news article from a media archive, and
a regional report from an international organization. The ministry's
robots.txt allows the crawler on its publications pages and nowhere else,
with a five-second delay; three of five candidate URLs are disallowed and
are not fetched.
:::

## Recipes

<Recipe title="5.1 List the sources and their terms" level="Foundational" skills="Dissemination officer; librarian" time="Half a day">

**Result:** the list of sites, archives, and datasets where policy,
media, and institutional use appears, with the method and the terms for
each.

1. Policy: ministries' publication pages, the parliament's records and
   questions, the budget and planning documents, subnational government
   sites, and the strategies of sector agencies.
2. International organizations: country reports and strategies of the
   World Bank, the IMF, UN agencies, and regional bodies, which quote
   national statistics with attribution.
3. Media: the national news archives, press monitoring the organization
   already subscribes to, and news datasets with free access
   ([GDELT](https://www.gdeltproject.org/) indexes world news with
   themes and sources and offers raw files and queries).
4. Web at large: [Common Crawl](https://commoncrawl.org/) holds periodic
   crawls of the public web with an index that can be queried by domain,
   which gives breadth without crawling sites directly.
5. For each source, record: method (export, feed, API, crawl), the
   robots.txt rules and terms of use, rate limit, and the licence of the
   content (government documents are often open; news is not).

**What this does not do.** The list is the scope. Sources whose terms do
not allow collection stay out, and the report states it; a press
monitoring service under contract covers part of that gap.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="5.2 Check robots.txt and crawl within the rules" level="AI-ready" skills="Developer" time="One day to set up; scheduled runs after">

**Result:** a crawler that fetches only what each site allows, identifies
itself, and waits as the site asks, with the check run before every
fetch.

```python file=../../website/static/cookbook-files/monitoring-data-use/robots_check.py
```

```bash
python robots_check.py robots_example.txt web_sources.csv --agent ai4data-monitor
```

Output for the running example:

```text
user agent 'ai4data-monitor': crawl delay 5
allowed   https://ministry.example.gov/publications/strategy-2025.pdf  (policy document)
allowed   https://ministry.example.gov/publications/  (document list)
DISALLOWED  https://ministry.example.gov/search?q=nutrition  (search page)
DISALLOWED  https://ministry.example.gov/news/2025/09/budget-speech  (news)
DISALLOWED  https://ministry.example.gov/api/internal/documents.json  (internal API)
sitemaps: https://ministry.example.gov/sitemap.xml

5 URLs, 3 disallowed
```

1. Fetch each site's robots.txt and run the check on the candidate URLs
   with the crawler's own user-agent token; fetch only the allowed ones
   and wait the stated delay between requests (the example asks for five
   seconds for this crawler and ten for others).
2. Identify the crawler: a user agent with the organization's name and a
   contact URL, so that a site administrator can see who is fetching and
   write to them.
3. Prefer the site's document list, feed, or sitemap (the example
   declares one) to crawling pages, and fetch the documents only.
4. Keep the fetched robots.txt and the decision with each document, so
   that a later question about a document's collection can be answered.
5. Where the robots.txt disallows what the organization needs, ask: a
   ministry usually grants a statistical office access on request, and an
   export is often easier for both.

**What this does not do.** robots.txt states what crawlers may fetch; the
terms of use and the licence of the content are read separately and
recorded in the sources list. The check is a gate, and a pipeline stops on
a disallowed URL.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="5.3 Use news and web datasets for breadth" level="AI-ready" skills="Developer" time="Two days to set up">

**Result:** mentions in news and on the web beyond the sites the
organization crawls, from datasets that already collected them.

1. GDELT: query its event and mention tables (in BigQuery or from the raw
   files) for the canonical names and the organization's name, and
   collect the article URLs and metadata; fetch the article text only
   where the publisher's terms allow.
2. Common Crawl: query the index for the domains in scope (ministries,
   parliament, international organizations) and read the pages from the
   crawl archives, which avoids crawling those sites at all.
3. Press monitoring: export the matches from the service the organization
   subscribes to, with the same document fields.
4. Store each document with its source dataset and the crawl date, and
   deduplicate against the directly collected documents by URL.

**What this does not do.** News datasets cover online news in the
languages and outlets they index; national-language regional press may be
absent. The coverage measure per source type shows the gap.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="5.4 Produce the text layer and measure coverage" level="AI-ready" skills="Developer; dissemination officer" time="One day to set up">

**Result:** every collected document has text the extractor can read, and
a measured share of known policy and media uses that the collection
contains.

1. Extract text from PDFs with PyMuPDF and from HTML with a readability
   step (main text without navigation); run OCR on scanned government
   documents ([documents cookbook, chapter 2](/cookbook/data-from-documents/scanned)).
2. Build a reference list of known policy and media uses: documents staff
   know, press clippings, and the uses the organization's own
   communications team tracked.
3. Measure coverage per source type (policy, international, media, web)
   and report it with the counts; add sources where it is low.

**What this does not do.** Unknown uses in unlisted sources remain
unknown; coverage is a lower bound per source type.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Collection:** document lists, sitemaps, feeds, and exports first; a crawler with the robots check, identification, and delays where needed; Common Crawl for breadth without direct crawling.
- **News:** GDELT for world news metadata; press monitoring exports; national archives' search exports.
- **Text:** PyMuPDF for PDFs; a readability extractor for HTML; OCR for scans.
- **Storage:** documents with source, URL, crawl date, licence, and the robots decision.

## World Bank examples

- [Monitoring of Data Use](/docs/data_use/) runs its extraction over policy documents and reports as well as papers, with the same text layer.
- The World Bank's country reports and strategies cite national statistical organizations by name, which makes them a reliable source of attributed policy use.

## Resources

- [RFC 9309, the Robots Exclusion Protocol](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309): the rules the check implements (group matching, longest path, crawl delay by convention).
- [Python `urllib.robotparser`](https://docs.python.org/3/library/urllib.robotparser.html): the standard library parser used in this chapter.
- [GDELT](https://www.gdeltproject.org/): a free, open news monitoring dataset with raw files and BigQuery access.
- [Common Crawl](https://commoncrawl.org/): periodic open crawls of the web with a queryable index by domain.
- [Scrapy](https://scrapy.org/): an open-source crawling framework with robots.txt obedience, delays, and identification built in, for the AI-ready level.
- [trafilatura](https://github.com/adbar/trafilatura): main-text extraction from HTML pages, for news and government web pages.

## Common mistakes

- **Crawling against the terms.** APIs, exports, and sitemaps exist for most sources; a disallowed path stays unfetched.
- **An anonymous crawler.** Sites block what they cannot identify; a contact URL in the user agent opens doors.
- **Crawling every page.** The documents are the evidence; the pages around them are load on the site.
- **Research-only collections.** They miss the policy use that justifies a statistical office.

## Verification

- **Sources:** each listed source has a recorded method, terms, and licence.
- **Crawler:** a disallowed URL is never fetched (the check runs before every fetch and the log shows no disallowed URL); the delay is respected.
- **Datasets:** GDELT or Common Crawl queries return documents for the domains in scope, deduplicated against direct collection.
- **Coverage:** the share of the reference list found, per source type, reported with the latest run.
