# 7. Harmonization of mentions

**Question:** Can mentions be matched to the datasets they refer to?

## Rationale

"Household Budget Survey 2021", "HBS", "the budget survey", and "the
household survey of 2021" are one dataset. A count by mention text spreads
one survey's use over a dozen rows and hides it. Harmonization maps each
mention to a canonical identifier, with a match type and a score, and
leaves what it cannot match for a person to review. The review is where
the names table of [chapter 1](./define.mdx) grows.

## Target state

- Every mention carries a dataset identifier, a match type, and a score, or is marked unmatched.
- Editions and vintages are distinguished where the typology needs it (the 2021 survey from the 2016 one).
- Other organizations' datasets found alongside are kept as co-use, and unmatched mentions are reviewed on a schedule.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Match mentions to the canonical names and acronyms exactly; review the rest by hand. |
| **AI-ready** | Match by exact, phrase, and fuzzy rules against the table with variants. Review unmatched and low-score matches each run and add variants. Track other organizations' data as co-use. |
| **AI-native** | Add semantic matching and clustering (the program's harmonization step) for new variants, distinguish editions, and feed confirmed variants back to the table automatically. |

:::info[Running example]
Of the twelve mentions, string matching resolves ten to the organization's
datasets: eight exactly and two by a phrase inside the mention. The two unmatched mentions are other organizations' data (the
Demographic and Health Survey and the World Development Indicators), which
the report keeps as co-use.
:::

<Recipe title="7.2 Distinguish editions and track other organizations' data" level="AI-ready" skills="Dissemination officer" time="Part of each review">

**Result:** uses counted at the right level (the survey series or a
specific round) and co-use with other datasets recorded.

1. Decide per dataset whether to count at the series level (the Labour
   Force Survey) or the edition level (the 2021 Household Budget Survey),
   and give both levels identifiers in the table, with the edition as a
   child of the series.
2. Match a mention with a year to the edition, and a mention without one
   to the series; report both levels.
3. Keep unmatched mentions of other organizations' datasets as **co-use**:
   which external data the organization's data are used with. This is
   informative for partnerships and for the gap analysis in
   [chapter 9](./act.mdx).
4. When another organization's dataset recurs, add it to a separate table
   with its own canonical name, so that co-use is counted consistently.

**What this does not do.** Editions distinguished by year in the text are
the easy case. A mention that names the series while the analysis used one
round is resolved only by reading the methods section, which the review
sample does.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="7.3 Review unmatched and low-score matches" level="AI-ready" skills="Dissemination officer" time="One hour per run">

**Result:** every unmatched or uncertain mention decided, and the names
table extended with the confirmed variants.

1. List the unmatched mentions and the phrase and fuzzy matches below a
   chosen score; the matcher prints both.
2. For each, decide: a variant of the organization's dataset (add it to the
   table), another organization's dataset (co-use), or a false positive
   from extraction (add the sentence to the labelled sample as a negative
   example).
3. Re-run the matcher; the mentions now match exactly.
4. Keep the decisions with the date and the person; disagreements between
   reviewers refine the rules.

**What this does not do.** Review resolves the mentions found. It does not
raise the extractor's recall; misses are addressed through the labelled
sample in [chapter 6](./detect.mdx).

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **String rules:** exact, phrase containment, and similarity ratio (difflib in this guide; RapidFuzz in the program's pipeline).
- **Semantic matching:** sentence embeddings and clustering, as in the program's `harmonize_datasets` step, for variants that string rules miss.
- **Identifiers:** series and edition identifiers from the catalog; DOIs where registered.
- **Review:** a queue of unmatched and low-score matches per run, with decisions recorded.

## Resources

- [RapidFuzz](https://github.com/rapidfuzz/RapidFuzz): fast string similarity for the production version of the matching in this chapter.
- [Sentence Transformers](https://www.sbert.net/): embeddings for semantic matching of variants that string rules miss, as in the program's `harmonize_datasets`.
- [Monitoring of Data Use](/docs/data_use/): the harmonization step of the program's pipeline and its outputs.
- [Metadata Reviewer review board](/docs/metadata-reviewer/user-manual/review-board): the review pattern for the unmatched and low-score queue.
- [SKOS](https://www.w3.org/TR/skos-reference/): preferred and alternate labels, the standard form of a canonical names table with variants.

## Common mistakes

- **Matching too eagerly.** A low fuzzy threshold matches "food security report" to the organization's report when the sentence meant any country's; the review sample catches it.
- **Discarding unmatched mentions.** They are the co-use signal and the source of new variants.
- **One level for all datasets.** A quarterly survey and a decennial census need different levels of counting.
- **Review without a record.** The same decision is made again next run.

## Verification

- **Match rate:** the share of mentions matched, by type, per run, with the unmatched reviewed.
- **Sample check:** a sample of phrase and fuzzy matches confirmed by a person; the error rate recorded.
- **Table growth:** variants added per run, which falls over time as the table matures.
